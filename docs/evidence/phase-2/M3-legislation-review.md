## Data flows touched
- **Worker to www.legislation.gov.uk:** `discover` fetches `https://www.legislation.gov.uk/{type}/data.feed?title={term}&sort={sort}` for each term, then the feed's `rel="next"` links, through `ctx.fetcher` (`_CountingFetcher`, then `CaptureFetcher`, then `SafeFetcher`). `fetch` then requests each entry's `data.xml`, or its HTML page. SafeFetcher follows up to 5 redirects to any public host.
- **Atom to candidates:** `url` (the entry link), `title`, `summary`, `external_id` (the entry id), `published_at`, `language` (`en`) and `hints["fetch"]` go to the run manifest, M4, users' browsers, emails and exports.
- **Worker to the object store:** the fetched body is stored under `raw/{source_id}/{sha256}`, and the manifest under `raw/{source_id}/runs/{run_id}.json`. Feed pages themselves are not stored.
- **Settings to requests:** `source.config.params` (written by holders of `sources.manage`) choose the type, terms, sort, pages per term and format. `general.contact_email` and `capture.user_agent` (admin settings) make up the User-Agent.
- **Errors:** `ConnectorError(reason, detail)` goes to `capture_run.error_class` and `error_message`, and into dry-run warnings. The sources screen maps `needs-contact` to a fixed text.
- **Trust boundary:** the feed bodies, statuses and headers, and anything they redirect to, are hostile (8.1).

| 1 | medium | **R-06's `data.pdf` and `data.docx` exclusion and its Crawl-delay 5 depend entirely on robots.txt being read and respected. The adapter enforces neither.** The module docstring (`legislation.py:11-15`) says that the per-host limiter "takes the slower of the job's rate and the Crawl-delay". That is true only while `RobotsPolicy.check` returns the delay. **Failure cases:** (a) **Override.** With `robots_policy=override`, `check` returns `None` (`robots.py:144-147`), so `interval(rate, None)` is the job's rate alone (`capture.py:63-66`). `test_probe.py` shows the intervals `[5.0, 5.0]` with robots.txt respected but `[0.5, 0.5]` with the override at the allowed job rate of 120 a minute. R-06 quotes the fair use policy: "You must follow the rules in the site's robots.txt file", so for this provider an override breaks the terms, not just etiquette. (b) **robots.txt answers 4xx**, a WAF 403 for example. All paths are then allowed and there is no Crawl-delay (`robots.py:129-130`). (c) **The adapter accepts PDF targets.** An entry whose link is `.../data.pdf` becomes the candidate URL and the fetch target (`legislation.py:158-164`). A `data.xml` link written `.../data.pdf#/data.xml` passes `_data_xml`'s check on the raw `href` (`legislation.py:114`). `_own` then drops the fragment, so the target is `https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1/made/data.pdf` (`test_probe.py`, FRAG). While robots.txt is respected, CaptureFetcher refuses these as robots skips (`partly-disallowed`). Under (a) or (b) they are fetched and stored. **Fix:** in the adapter, (1) refuse any target whose path contains `/data.pdf` or `/data.docx` (robots.txt's `*/data.pdf` matches anywhere in the path), and run `_data_xml`'s `/data.xml` check on the link `_own` returns, not on the raw `href`; (2) pass `rate_per_minute=min(job.rate_per_minute, 12)` for every request, so 5 s holds whatever robots.txt says; (3) refuse `robots_policy=override` for `legislation_gov_uk` sources at save, or at least at run time, citing E-UK-3. ## Checked and found fine
- **Request shape:** the origin is the constant `https://www.legislation.gov.uk` and the path is `/{type}/data.feed`, with `type` restricted to lowercase letters (but see finding 6a). `title` and `sort` go through `urlencode`, so a term such as `a&page=9#` stays one encoded value. The path and query match R-06 (`/all/data.feed?title=...&sort=published`, E-UK-7, E-UK-10). `source.url` is not used, and `endpoint_url` is refused.
- **Paging:** only the feed's `rel="next"` link is followed, and no page number is computed. It must be https on `www.legislation.gov.uk` on the default port with the same path, and not a URL already asked for in this term. At most `max_pages_per_term` pages (1 to 5) are read per term. A next link left after the cap adds `truncated`. An off-path or repeated next link stops discovery with `malformed-answer`; with documents already fetched, the run is `partial` and keeps them.
- **Contact before any request:** `needs-contact` is raised before the first fetch, and the test asserts that no path was requested. The agent is found through `_CountingFetcher.inner` and `CaptureFetcher.fetcher` down to `SafeFetcher.user_agent`, which is what is sent. It fails closed when not found.
- **Provider limit:** the job's rate is at most 120 a minute (`schemas.py:48`). Slots are per host in Valkey, shared by all workers and sources, so even without Crawl-delay a host gets at most 600 requests in 5 minutes, under the 1,500 limit. With robots.txt respected, the interval is 5 s (`test_probe.py`: `[0.5, 5.0, 5.0]`, the first being the robots.txt fetch).
- **Feed parsing:** each page goes through `rss._parse`: the 5 MiB, depth, element, DOCTYPE and entity checks, then feedparser from an in-memory stream, in a thread. Answers that are not feeds and error statuses are refused before anything is stored.
- **Candidate values:** `title` and `summary` go through `plain_text`, `external_id` through `identifier`, `published_at` through `entry_time`, and `language` is the constant `en`. `url` and `hints["fetch"]` pass `_own`: `entry_link` (SafeFetcher's `parse_target`, backslashes, invisible controls, numeric, percent-encoded and local hosts) plus https, the default port and the exact host. So no stored link names another host or a private address. `fetch` checks `hints["fetch"]` with `_own` again, and falls back to the checked candidate URL.
- **Duplicates across terms:** one `seen` set over all terms holds both links and ids, so an entry found by two terms gives one candidate (tested).
- **Storage:** the key is `raw/{source_id}/{sha256}`, with `source_id` from the job row, so nothing from the feed shapes it. Error pages are not stored. Equal bodies share an object.
- **Errors:** every `ConnectorError` detail is a constant, an integer status, or the first 50 characters of an admin-written key. `FetchRefusedError` details are replaced by fixed text in `run_connector` and in the dry run.
- **Save time:** `check_params` runs `legislation.check` from `services.check_urls`, and the integration case gets a 422 on `config.params`. Unknown keys are refused. `max_pages_per_term` refuses booleans and values outside 1 to 5, and `title_terms` needs 1 to 20 strings of at most 100 characters. The same check runs again at the start of each run, on the stored settings.
- **Dry run:** it stops after 5 candidates or its deadline, stores nothing, and shows `needs-contact` as a fixed warning.
