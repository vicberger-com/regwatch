## Data flows touched
- **Captured body to tree:** a fetched body (bytes, hostile) and the charset label the server sent go to `tree.parse`. html5lib decodes the first 2 MiB (the transport label, else BOM, `<meta>` prescan or windows-1252) and tokenizes it. The capped tree builder then builds an `xml.etree` tree. `Page.root` is the `html` element, and `cut` says whether the body was truncated.
- **Rules to selectors:** holders of `sources.manage` save `content_selectors`, `exclude_selectors`, `title_selector` and `date_selector`. These are compiled at save time (`compile_selector`) and again at run time.
- **Tree and selector to elements:** `Selector.select(root)` walks the tree once per call (`elements`) and returns matches in document order. Text extraction (not in this slice) will read them.
- **Errors:** `ParseError(reason, detail)` has fixed detail text with no page content. `SelectorError` carries the selector text (admin input) and up to 20 characters of it. Any other exception would end a capture run as `internal-error`.
- **Trust boundary:** the body and the charset label are hostile (8.1). Selectors are admin input, checked on save.

## Checked and found fine
- **Depth and push caps:** `reset` replaces `openElements` after `super().reset()`, and html5lib never assigns `openElements` anywhere else. Every `insertElement` path (normal, table and foster parenting, reconstruction) goes through `append`. `<div>` × 100,000 and `<b>` × 100,000 are refused as `too-deep` in 0.02 s, and 2 MiB of `<br>` is refused as `too-large` in 1.1 s at 137 MiB. Every adoption agency pattern tried that nests is refused as `too-deep` within 0.1 s. The 2 MiB cut is applied to the bytes before decoding, and `cut` is reported.
- **Exceptions:** `ParseError` raised inside html5lib is not caught by html5lib (only `_ReparseException` is), so it reaches the caller. Across 26 labels (unknown, empty, NUL, `replacement`, `utf-7`, `utf-16`, `x-user-defined`, ISO-2022 and others) × 40 bodies (BOMs, late and odd `<meta charset>`, surrogate and out-of-range character references, foreign content, `<template>`, `<frameset>`, `<plaintext>`, NUL bytes), and 20,000 random tag-soup bodies, nothing raised other than `ParseError`, apart from the surrogate label (finding 6d). Unknown labels fall back to sniffing.
- **Walk:** `elements` is iterative, skips comments and processing instructions (function tags), and numbers element children only, so `:first-child`, `:last-child` and `:nth-child` ignore comments and text, as in CSS. The root is `html`, and the doctype and comments outside it are not in the tree.
- **Selector compile cost:** with a 300-character limit, the worst cases tried (unclosed `:nth-child(`, `[`, `:not(`, long names, quoted values, `.a` × 150, `*` × 300, digit runs) compile in under 0.07 ms. `_TOKEN` and `_NTH` have no nested quantifiers that could backtrack exponentially, and `int` on up to 288 digits is far below Python's 4,300-digit limit.
- **Selector compile checks:** 100,000 random selectors raised only `SelectorError`. A leading or trailing combinator, a trailing comma, an empty `:not()`, nested `:not`, a combinator inside `:not`, `|=`, `+`, `~`, pseudo-elements, more than 10 alternatives or 8 steps, and non-strings are all refused. A type selector after a class is refused. Tag and attribute names are lowered, which is right for HTML elements.
- **An+B arithmetic:** `_nth` is right for a = 0 (index = b), negative a (`-n+3` gives 1 to 3, `-2n+5` gives 1, 3, 5), negative b (`3n-2` gives 1, 4, 7; `-n-1` gives none), `0n+0` (none) and `n+0` (all). Python's floor division and modulo with a negative a give the right sign test, and large a or b only make the set empty.
- **Attribute operators:** `[a=""]` matches an empty value. `[a~=""]`, `[a^=""]`, `[a$=""]` and `[a*=""]` match nothing, as Selectors 4 says. `~=` with a space in the value matches nothing. `#a#b` needs both. An id matches the whole value.
- **Matching algorithm:** for step i, the child combinator reads the parent's own bits, and the descendant combinator reads the bits of the parent and its ancestors, so the result is the same as right-to-left matching, with no backtracking (checked on `div:not(#main) p`, `* > section *`, `body > p` after foster parenting, and `table > tr`, which is empty because `tbody` is implied). `id()` keys are stable because `walk` keeps every element alive. Results are de-duplicated across alternatives and returned in document order.
- **Dependency records:** pins `html5lib>=1.1,<2` and `webencodings>=0.5.1,<0.6`, with exact versions and sha256 hashes in `uv.lock`. Both are pure Python (`py2.py3-none-any`), with no native code. six 1.17.0 is already C15. The register's former trafilatura and readability-lxml rows are kept, marked "not used". The `webencodings` exception has `scope = "any"` and BSD-3-Clause, which is on the allow-list, and it is used by the scan (no "unused" warning). html5lib's wheel ships its LICENSE and AUTHORS.

**Second slice (`990165b`), checked and found fine:**
- `text_of` is iterative. Skipped and boilerplate elements drop their content but keep their tails, and comments contribute only their tails.
- Block elements make line breaks and cells make spaces. `a<b>b</b>c` stays `abc`, and source line breaks become spaces.

**Caller guidance for the web_page connector:**
- **Concurrency:** run at most one `extract_isolated` per worker process at a time, for example with a semaphore. Keep the worker concurrency at or below the core count, so the 20 s wall clock is reached only by a child that is really slow. Peak memory is about 1 GiB times that concurrency, and the host must hold it.
- **Run budget:** a hostile page costs up to about 10 s of CPU or 20 s of wall clock, plus about 0.3 s of start-up per document. A run that extracts N documents can therefore take up to N × 20 s, and Celery's soft limit is 3,300 s. Cap the documents per run. Stop extracting from a source, and record it, after a few `too-slow` or `too-large` results in one run.
- **Stay isolated:** keep every call of `tree.parse`, `text.extract` and `Selector.select` on captured pages behind `extract_isolated`. Their in-process bounds do not hold (finding 1), and a test or import check could enforce this.
- **Before the connector lands:** kill the child on cancellation (R1). Trim the body before sending it (R5a).
