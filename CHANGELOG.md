# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are recorded here. Format: Keep a Changelog; every line ends with issue and requirement IDs. A release section is cut at every phase gate.

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- Component register rows for M3, approved by the owner: protego 0.7.0 (robots.txt, BSD-3), tenacity 9.2.1 (retries, Apache-2.0) and redis-py 8.1.0 (Valkey client for rate limits and Celery, MIT), verified in R-01 addendum C1 to C3 (M3, REQ-20)
- M3 SafeFetcher, part 1, `regwatch.capture.safe_fetch.policy`: only http and https URLs without user information are fetchable, and only when every address their host resolves to is a public unicast address. Private, loopback, link-local, shared, reserved, multicast and documentation ranges are refused, including when embedded in IPv6 (IPv4-mapped, 6to4, Teredo, NAT64), and numeric spellings such as `2130706433` resolve to the same refusal. Any private answer among several refuses the host. `validate_source_url` is the check when a source is saved (M3, M3 acceptance 3, REQ-05)
- M3 SafeFetcher, part 2, `SafeFetcher.fetch`: each hop connects to an address it has just checked, with the host name in `Host` and as the TLS server name, so DNS cannot change the answer between check and connection; redirects are followed by hand, at most 5, each re-checked; bodies stop at 10 MB after decompression, so a gzip bomb or a lying `Content-Length` is refused; connect 10 s, read 30 s and a 60 s deadline per hop stop slow servers; environment proxies are ignored and connections are not reused, so each hop's certificate is checked for its own name. httpx is now a runtime dependency (M3, M3 acceptance 3, REQ-05)
- SafeFetcher review follow-ups: bodies are decompressed by SafeFetcher with an output limit on every step, so a nested gzip bomb stays within the 10 MB cap in memory, and stacked or unknown content encodings are refused; every IPv6 address in `::/64` (IPv4-compatible, -mapped and -translated forms) and site-local `fec0::/10` is refused; DNS lookups count against the per-hop deadline; the test-only host pinning moved out of the production class into a test subclass; TLS is shown to refuse a certificate for another name. After re-verification: data after the end of a compressed body is decoded as a further gzip member or refused, never left to pile up; raw bytes count against the cap too; truncated streams are refused and raw deflate is accepted; decoded bodies drop their encoding headers; a TLS context that checks less is refused; timeouts name DNS or the server (M3, REQ-05)
- M3 SafeFetcher, part 3, `RobotsPolicy`: robots.txt is read through SafeFetcher (512 KB cap), parsed with protego and cached per origin for 24 hours. A 4xx means everything is allowed and a 5xx or no answer means nothing is (RFC 9309), retried after 15 minutes. A disallowed URL is refused with reason `robots`, and the site's Crawl-delay is returned for the rate limiter. An admin override skips the rules only with a recorded reason (M3 acceptance 4, REQ-05)
- M3 SafeFetcher, part 4: per-host rate limits in Valkey, shared by every worker. One Lua script reserves each host's next slot using Valkey's clock. The interval is the slower of the job's rate (default 30 a minute) and the site's Crawl-delay, and a wait over 2 minutes is refused as `rate-limited`. `CaptureFetcher` checks robots.txt and takes the rate slot before every hop, including redirect targets, then fetches through SafeFetcher. A 200-page crawl at 10 a minute never makes more than 10 requests to the host in any minute (M3 acceptance 5, REQ-05)
- SafeFetcher slices 3 and 4 review follow-ups: a refused rate request reserves nothing, and a host's key lives until its last queued slot; Crawl-delay is clamped to an hour and intervals round up; robots.txt is cached in an LRU bounded by bytes, read up to 2 MiB and parsed to 500 KiB, and a 429 means try later; robots.txt fetches take a rate slot; the address check runs before robots and rate, so a private redirect is reported as such; acceptance 5 is tested against Valkey and the fixture server (M3, BL-87, REQ-05)
- SafeFetcher re-verification follow-ups (findings 23 to 27, residual 21): `check_url`'s DNS lookup has the hop's deadline; robots.txt longer than 500 KiB is cut at the last line break, so a half-read `Allow` can never widen to the whole site; concurrent first requests to a site fetch robots.txt once and reserve one rate slot for it; the rate limiters clamp intervals themselves and refuse a NaN, negative or infinite longest wait; CR-only robots.txt files are cut at a line break too; the acceptance 5 test checks every 1-second window of arrival times (M3, REQ-05)
- M3 connector protocol (`regwatch.capture.connectors`): `Candidate`, `RawDocument`, `HealthReport`, `JobSettings`, `ConnectorContext` and the `Connector` protocol, with one registered connector per kind. Connectors reach the network only through `CaptureFetcher`. Pydantic models for every kind's `source.config` and for `extraction_rules` mirror `docs/contracts/connectors.yaml`; a contract test checks properties, required fields, defaults, bounds and enums, and invalid settings answer 422 naming each field. ORM models for `source`, `capture_job` and `capture_run` are checked against the migrated tables (M3, REQ-02, REQ-04)
- M3 source and capture-job routes: `GET`, `POST`, `PATCH` and `DELETE /sources` (delete disables and keeps), `GET /capture-jobs` and `PATCH /capture-jobs/{id}`. Every URL a source names, its `url` and those in its config, must resolve to public addresses when saved (acceptance 3), and the answer never names the address. The config and extraction rules are checked against the connector contract, jurisdiction codes and term ids must exist, and a job has a cron or an interval. Setting `robots_policy=override` needs `sources.override_robots` and a reason of at most 1,000 characters, and is audited as `source.robots_override` (acceptance 4, review finding 22 (a)). Every change is audited (M3, REQ-02, REQ-05)
- M3 source routes review follow-ups: new URLs on an overridden source, or turning it back on, need `sources.override_robots` and are audited with the URLs; save-time lookups run together under a 10 s deadline before any row lock; refused URLs get one message whatever the name resolved to; the config is stored as checked; `terms_url` takes only http and https links; names and reasons refuse control and direction characters, and `language` takes a language tag; cron fields are range-checked and intervals are at most a year; job rows are locked with their source; `page` is at most 1,000,000 on every list. After re-verification: turning the job of an overridden source back on needs the override permission too; cron fields take ASCII digits only; save-time lookups use a thread pool of their own; an empty `url` is stored as none; a public IP literal is answered without the lookup pool, the resume audit row names the job's state, and the production resolver is covered by a test (M3, BL-88, REQ-05)
- M3 run history and source health: `GET /capture-runs` lists runs newest first, filtered by job and status, and `GET /sources/{id}/health` gives the 7-day run count, success rate, last success and the last error while it is still current (M3, REQ-02)
- feedparser 6.0.14 and feedparser-sgmllib 2.1.0 for the RSS connector, approved by the owner and verified in R-01 addendum C17 and C18 (M3, REQ-20)
- Celery 5.7.0 and its dependencies, approved by the owner and verified in R-01 addendum C4 to C16; the license scan records six wheels whose metadata says only 'BSD License' as reviewed corrections (M3, REQ-20)
- M3 job schedules (`regwatch.capture.schedule`): cron expressions with names, ranges, lists, steps and cron's either-day rule, computed in the job's time zone across daylight-saving changes. Saving a job sets `next_run_at`; a schedule that never fires is refused (M3, REQ-02)
- M3 scheduler: `regwatch-worker` and `regwatch-beat` run Celery (JSON messages only, acknowledged after the task, no result backend). Beat fires `capture.schedule_tick` every minute and `capture.health_check` hourly. The tick starts every due job of an enabled source with up to 30 s of jitter, at most one unfinished run per source. `POST /capture-jobs/{id}/run-now` queues a run and audits it. Runs end with the source's health updated; until a kind has a connector its runs fail as `no-connector`, and runs that never finish are ended after 2 hours (M3, ADR-0006, REQ-02)
- M3 scheduler review follow-ups: Beat keeps its schedule in memory, so no pickle file is read at start; broker calls have 5 s timeouts, a bounded retry and a 15 s deadline on their own threads; remote control is off and a dying worker's task is not redelivered without end; a job with a broken schedule fails alone; lost messages are handed over again up to three times, as ADR-0006 says, and our own failures no longer count against a source; the sweep ends runs in one transaction and never overwrites an ended run; cron times never go back during a repeated hour; run-now coalesces calls within 60 s and refuses a disabled job; error messages are cleaned to one printable line. After re-verification: every tick also ends abandoned runs, so a Beat restarted more often than hourly cannot strand a job, and a retry after a refused hand-over queues a new run (M3, BL-90, REQ-05)
